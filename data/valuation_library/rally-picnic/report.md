## Valuation Interpretation

The evidence cluster is broad rather than tight. Three of the five comparables fall between approximately **$22,600 and $32,800**, while the remaining two reach **$44,450** and **$56,250**. That wide range is reflected in the model's **102.8% dispersion** measure.

The engine's weighting system substantially reduces the influence of the $56,250 high-side sale, but the evidence still does not support a narrow valuation band. The resulting **$22,579-$44,450** fair-value range should therefore be interpreted as a broad research range rather than a precise appraisal interval.

The **$32,760 base case** sits close to the center of the stronger evidence and is also directly anchored by a verified 2025 USD sale.

## Key Risks and Limitations

The valuation carries three formal warnings:

- **Category model is provisional and requires methodological review.**
- **Extreme comparable dispersion.**
- **Valuation completed with optional evidence limitations.**

The principal asset-specific limitation is **unknown subject condition**. Barénia leather develops patina and can show scratching, while wicker condition can vary materially through breaks, discoloration, or deformation. No condition premium or discount was applied because the available subject data does not support one.

Buyer-premium treatment is also not fully normalized across the comparable set.

Two foreign-currency comparables were converted using the engine's configured static FX rates rather than transaction-date FX rates. This is acceptable within the current methodology but remains an approximation.

A further methodology caveat is that the research preserves year-only sale information for certain comparables, while the valuation engine's recency calculation depends on an exact `sale_date`. Where an exact date is absent, the current engine can effectively apply full recency weight. That should be reviewed in a future methodology revision because it may overstate the recency of year-only observations.
